\begin{abstract}
Consumer editing tools make local image manipulation trivial, yet passive forensic detectors return opaque scores, need annotated training corpora and GPU inference, and cannot restore what was destroyed. Recent learned proactive watermarks (e.g.\ EditGuard, DeepMark) now both localize and recover tampering, so ``detects and recovers'' is no longer distinguishing. This paper offers a self-embedding fragile watermark that is instead deterministic, training-free, and cryptographically auditable: every verification decision is a single keyed HMAC comparison any third party holding the key can recompute bit-for-bit. Each $8\times8$ block carries, per channel, a 32-bit keyed tag over its own six most-significant bit-planes and the recovery descriptor it holds, plus a 96-bit descriptor of a spatially distant partner block chosen by a key-seeded single-cycle permutation, all in two least-significant bit-planes. Verification recomputes each tag; a mismatch localizes tampering, and each flagged block is rebuilt from its partner's descriptor or marked unrecoverable. LSB shifting raises imperceptibility to 45.479/45.492~dB (SSIM minimum 0.98031), closing most of the gap to a leading published scheme's 45.57~dB. Across 1,792 main-grid tamper trials (four classes, seven ratios, 10--70\%), block-level recall is 1.0000 for three of four classes and 0.999981 (minimum 0.998498) for object-removal-by-inpainting -- the one class where a smooth fill can reproduce a block's own most-significant bits, the predicted failure mode appearing in measurement for the first time, on 22 blocks. Headline localization is pixel-level precision, 0.9614; block-level precision is not reported since the ground-truth rule makes it structurally indistinguishable from 1.0. A 1,802,240-block null condition yields zero false positives. The recoverability rate $\rho$ tracks its structural bound $1-\alpha$ closely, staying 3--6 points above it at every one of seven measured ratios, refuting an earlier three-point grid's apparent cliff: coverage degrades smoothly, not catastrophically. In-region recovery PSNR stays flat at 29.3--29.8~dB (mean 29.00~dB) across the range while whole-image PSNR falls with shrinking coverage -- coverage, not descriptor quality, sets the recovery ceiling, and one-to-one partner mapping still falls short of reference-sharing schemes at high ratios.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The evidentiary value of a digital image rests on an assumption that is increasingly unsafe: that the pixels presented are the pixels captured. Region-level manipulation -- pasting an object from one photograph into another, erasing an object with an inpainting tool, or replacing a cropped region with plausible texture -- is now available in free software and, with diffusion-based generative editors, requires neither skill nor time. A radiological study with a lesion removed, a vehicle damage photograph submitted in an insurance claim, a surveillance frame entered into a legal record, or a remote-sensing tile in an environmental dispute all derive their utility from integrity the image format itself does not protect.

Two broad families of countermeasures exist. \emph{Passive} (blind) forensics analyses the received image alone for statistical inconsistencies in noise residuals, compression history, colour filter array traces, illumination or resampling artefacts; modern instances are learned, using CNN or transformer backbones to output a per-pixel manipulation heat map \cite{zhou2018learning,wu2019mantranet}. \emph{Active} approaches insert authentication information before distribution, so verification becomes self-consistency, not provenance inference. Fragile and semi-fragile watermarking belongs to this second family \cite{linchang2000,rey2002survey}.

Even a perfect detector answers only half the question a practitioner asks. Knowing \emph{that}, or even precisely \emph{which}, pixels of a radiograph were altered does not restore the diagnostic content overwritten. In the passive paradigm, restoration is necessarily generative: an inpainting network hallucinates content that is plausible but has no evidentiary relationship to the original -- worse than no restoration when the artefact is evidence. What is required is a mechanism by which the \emph{original} content, even a coarse low-frequency approximation, survives the attack and can be reinstated.

Self-embedding watermarking, introduced by Fridrich and Goljan \cite{fridrich1999selfembedding,fridrich1999selfcorrecting}, provides exactly this: a compressed representation of each region is hidden elsewhere \emph{within the same image}, so a spatially localized attack destroys the region but not its backup. Our system develops this into a complete, reproducible localization-and-recovery pipeline and evaluates both capabilities \emph{together}, which the literature has tended not to do.

A second motivation is deployment economics. Learned tamper detectors require large annotated datasets, accelerator hardware at inference time -- excluding embedded acquisition devices, hospital PACS gateways and field equipment -- and produce outputs not explainable in a form that survives adversarial cross-examination; a per-pixel softmax score of 0.78 is not an argument. By contrast, the scheme here is classical deterministic signal processing: every positive detection is the statement ``the 32-bit keyed tag stored in block $i$ does not equal the tag recomputed from block $i$'s current most-significant content,'' a verifiable, repeatable, auditable fact, with no training set, no GPU, and no run-to-run variance.

The contributions of this paper are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A self-embedding fragile watermarking design separating \emph{authentication} payload (in-block) from \emph{recovery} payload (in a distant partner block), fitting entirely within two LSB planes and bounding embedding distortion analytically.
\item A construction avoiding the \emph{tamper coincidence problem}: a key-seeded single-cycle permutation over the block index set, built in cycle notation so bijectivity and the absence of short cycles hold unconditionally, with an enforced minimum-separation repair pass. The distant-mapping principle is AuSR3's \cite{ausr3}; the construction is ours.
\item A joint evaluation protocol over four tamper classes at seven ratios with exact, self-generated ground truth, reporting imperceptibility, localization, recoverability rate, and in-region recovery PSNR/SSIM together, so the localization--recovery trade-off is visible rather than implied.
\item Explicit positioning against classical self-embedding and recent learned proactive watermarking, which now also localizes \emph{and} recovers \cite{editguard,deepmark,recovermark}: the axis of difference is determinism, zero training/GPU inference, and full explainability, not accuracy superiority, with the scheme's core limitation -- fragility to any lossy re-encoding -- stated candidly.
\item An adversarial security analysis surfacing and fixing two real defects with verified exploits: an unauthenticated recovery descriptor (96 of 128 payload bits unprotected) and a block-mapping seed order leaking spatial structure beyond its stated guarantee. Both fixed at zero payload cost and verified against the exploit that found them.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Robust versus fragile watermarking.} Early watermarking research favoured \emph{robustness} against compression, filtering and geometric distortion so ownership could be asserted, canonically Cox \emph{et al.}'s spread-spectrum formulation \cite{cox1997secure}. Authentication inverts the requirement -- the watermark should be \emph{fragile}, failing under modification, because failure is the signal. Wong and Memon \cite{wong2001secret} developed block-wise fragile schemes with per-block LSB signatures, computable without the original image; see \cite{rey2002survey} for a survey.

\textbf{Semi-fragile authentication.} A purely fragile watermark cannot distinguish a malicious splice from a benign JPEG re-save. Lin and Chang \cite{linchang2000} exploit an invariant DCT-coefficient-pair relationship preserved by quantization but broken by manipulation; cited to delimit scope, since this system is deliberately fully fragile (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\textbf{Self-embedding and content recovery.} The decisive step from detection to \emph{recovery} is due to Fridrich and Goljan, embedding a compressed version of each block within the image itself \cite{fridrich1999selfembedding,fridrich1999selfcorrecting}; their block-wise compression, key-dependent distant mapping, and LSB embedding is the direct ancestor of this design. Lin \emph{et al.} \cite{lin2005hierarchical} verify at several granularities simultaneously, informing Section~\ref{sec:method}'s neighbourhood post-processing. Zhang and Wang \cite{zhang2008fragile,zhang2009extensive} and Zhang \emph{et al.} \cite{zhang2011reference} share reference information across blocks so reconstruction solves a linear system, raising the tolerable tamper ratio above the one-to-one limit; Korus and Dziech \cite{korus2013efficient} give a rate--distortion analysis of reconstruction quality vs.\ tampering rate. Aminuddin and Ernawan's AuSR series \cite{ausr1,ausr2,ausr3} target finer $2\times2$ granularity and name the \emph{tamper coincidence problem}; AuSR3 \cite{ausr3} maps to the most distant available location, the principle Section~\ref{sec:method} implements as a single-cycle permutation with minimum-separation repair.

\textbf{Security of localized authentication watermarks.} Holliman and Memon \cite{holliman2000counterfeiting} showed that a block watermark depending only on its own content lets an adversary collage authentic blocks from several watermarked images into a \emph{counterfeit} that verifies everywhere; Fridrich \cite{fridrich2002security} analysed the same problem. The consequence, adopted below: the tag must bind block index and an image-level identifier under a secret key.

\textbf{Learned manipulation localization.} Passive, learning-based localization has advanced rapidly: two-stream RGB/noise-residual splice localization \cite{zhou2018learning} and self-supervised anomaly-feature forgery localization \cite{wu2019mantranet}, needing no acquisition-device cooperation at the cost of annotated training data, GPU inference, and non-explainable scores -- complementary to, not competing with, active watermarking.

\textbf{Learned proactive watermarking.} A more recent line embeds a learned watermark so a trained network can both localize and recover content. EditGuard \cite{editguard} jointly optimizes an invertible watermarking network, reporting over 95\% localization precision; DeepMark \cite{deepmark} detects and localizes across images and video; RecoverMark \cite{recovermark} targets recovery for manipulated faces. ``Detects only, does not recover'' is thus no longer accurate of active watermarking as a field; the distinction that survives is \emph{auditability} -- a learned localizer's output is only reproducible with the model weights, whereas every decision below is a single keyed HMAC comparison anyone holding the key can recompute by hand.

\textbf{Positioning.} Across the self-embedding literature, detection papers report localization accuracy and recovery papers report reconstruction PSNR under one attack, but few report both jointly over several tamper classes and ratios against exact ground truth -- this joint characterization, for a minimal training-free construction, is this paper's contribution.

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Notation and Preliminaries}

Let the host image be a grayscale array $I$ of size $M \times N$, 8-bit, $I(x,y) \in \{0,\dots,255\}$; colour images apply the procedure independently per channel. Spatial dimensions are assumed multiples of block size $B=8$; otherwise the image is cropped to the largest block-aligned region rather than padded, since padding would place real blocks' descriptors into discarded phantom blocks (no corpus image requires this). The image is partitioned into $K = (M/B)\cdot(N/B)$ non-overlapping blocks $\{\mathbf{b}_1,\dots,\mathbf{b}_K\}$ in raster order, $\mathbf{b}_i \in \{0,\dots,255\}^{B\times B}$; let $c(i) \in \mathbb{R}^2$ denote the pixel-coordinate centroid of block $i$.

Two bit-planes are reserved for embedding. Define the \textbf{MSB projection} operator, which zeroes the two least-significant bits of every pixel:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}) = \left\lfloor \mathbf{b} / 4 \right\rfloor \cdot 4
\label{eq:msb}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}) = \mathbf{b} \,\text{AND}\, \texttt{0xFC}$. All payload -- tag and descriptor -- is computed from $\mathrm{MSB}(\cdot)$ of the source block, never the raw block, so embedding cannot alter the quantity that was hashed or compressed; the verifier recomputes it from the received image without the original and without iterative search.

The per-block payload capacity is $2 \times B^2 = 128$ bits, allocated as
\begin{equation}
128 = \underbrace{32}_{\text{tag } a_i} + \underbrace{96}_{\text{descriptor } r_j,\ j=m^{-1}(i)}
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
so block $i$ carries its \emph{own} authentication tag and \emph{another} block's recovery descriptor. The 32-bit tag yields a per-block false-acceptance probability of $2^{-32}\approx 2.3\times10^{-10}$, negligible over the $K=4096$ blocks of a $512\times512$ image. \textbf{This 32+96 budget is per channel, not per block overall}: a colour image applies the full construction independently on each channel, so a colour block carries three independent 32-bit tags and three independent 96-bit descriptors (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), not one 128-bit payload shared across channels; Table~\ref{tab:falseaccept} gives the resulting multi-channel false-accept probability explicitly.

\subsection{Recovery Descriptor Construction}

For each block $i$, a 96-bit descriptor $r_i$ compressing the visual content of $\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}_i)$ is computed. Variants A and C form the main experimental grid; Variant B is an ablation only (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\textbf{Variant A (DCT, primary).} Let $\mathbf{C}_i = \mathrm{DCT2}(\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}_i) - 128)$ be the level-shifted 2-D type-II DCT of the block, $\sigma(1),\dots,\sigma(64)$ the zig-zag scan order, retaining the first $L=12$ coefficients (DC plus eleven lowest-frequency AC). Each retained coefficient is quantized to a \emph{uniform} 8 signed bits,
\begin{equation}
\hat{q}_{i,\ell} = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\mathrm{round}\!\left(\frac{C_i(\sigma(\ell))}{\Delta_\ell}\right), -128, 127\right),\; \ell=1,\dots,12,
\label{eq:quant}
\end{equation}
so $L\times8 = 12\times8 = 96$ bits exactly, with round-half-even rounding. The decreasing-precision intent of natural-image DCT coding is carried by the per-band step size $\Delta_\ell$ rather than a decreasing bit allocation: $\Delta_\ell$ is the JPEG quality-50 luminance table in zig-zag order, DC entry reduced to 8 so the highest-energy term keeps the finest step. Concatenating the 12 signed bytes in scan order gives $r_i \in \{0,1\}^{96}$, two's-complement, no explicit sign bit.

\emph{No retained coefficient can clip.} With an orthonormal 2-D DCT, Cauchy--Schwarz gives $|C_i(u,v)| \le 128B = 1024$ for $B=8$; the DC step of 8 gives $1024/8=128$ (exactly the signed 8-bit range) and every AC step is at least 10, giving $1024/10=102.4<127$ -- $\mathrm{clip}(\cdot)$ in \eqref{eq:quant} is a defensive bound only, never triggered. Reconstruction dequantizes, zero-pads coefficients $\ell>L$, applies the inverse DCT, and re-adds the level shift:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_i = \mathrm{clip}\big(\mathrm{IDCT2}(\mathcal{Z}(\hat{q}_i \odot \Delta)) + 128,\ 0,\ 255\big)
\label{eq:idct}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{Z}(\cdot)$ places values into an $8\times8$ zero array at the zig-zag positions.

\textbf{Variant B (mean-pooled, low-complexity).} The $8\times8$ block is reduced to a $4\times4$ array of $2\times2$ means,
\begin{equation}
\mu_i(u,v) = \left\lfloor \frac{1}{4}\sum_{p=0}^{1}\sum_{q=0}^{1} \mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}_i)(2u+p, 2v+q) \right\rfloor
\label{eq:meanpool}
\end{equation}
for $0 \le u,v \le 3$, each quantized to 6 bits by discarding the two least-significant bits, $\hat{\mu}_i = \lfloor \mu_i/4 \rfloor$, giving exactly $16\times6=96$ bits. Reconstruction upsamples $4\hat{\mu}_i+2$ to $8\times8$. Variant B needs only integer shifts, no transform, and targets microcontroller-class deployment, but measurably trails A in reconstruction fidelity (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\textbf{Variant C (adaptive DCT, main-grid default).} Variant C spends the same 96-bit budget as Variant A on the same DCT coefficients but chooses per block between two candidate bit-allocation tables. One bit of the 96 records which table was used; both candidates are reconstructed (dequantize, IDCT, clip, project to MSB) and compared against the target block in \emph{pixel} space -- the reconstruction path is non-linear -- and the lower-squared-error table is kept, at the cost of one bit of descriptor budget and one extra trial reconstruction at embed time; decode-time cost is unchanged. Measured, paired by (image, tamper class, ratio, key, seed) on the full grid, Variant C's in-region recovery gain over Variant A has mean $+2.63$~dB, worst case $+1.40$~dB (\emph{tiffany}), best case $+3.83$~dB (\emph{kodim08}), with zero images regressing.

In all three variants, the descriptor is a function of $\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}_i)$ only, so the two-LSB uncertainty of the recovered block is inherently bounded.

\subsection{Keyed Authentication Tag}

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be SHA-256 \cite{fips180-4} and $K_s$ a secret key. Let $\mathrm{ID}$ be an image-level identifier (nonce, capture timestamp, or device serial) stored alongside the image. The authentication tag of block $i$ is
\begin{equation}
a_i = \mathrm{Trunc}_{32}\Big(\mathrm{HMAC}_{K_s}\big(\mathrm{ID} \,\Vert\, i \,\Vert\, \mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}_i) \,\Vert\, r_{m^{-1}(i)}\big)\Big)
\label{eq:hmac}
\end{equation}
where $\Vert$ denotes concatenation, $\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}_i)$ is serialized in raster order, and $r_{m^{-1}(i)}$ is the recovery descriptor block $i$ itself physically carries (Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget}). Dependence on $\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}_i)$ means embedding into LSBs does not invalidate the tag; on $i$ and $\mathrm{ID}$ defends against block-wise collage counterfeiting \cite{holliman2000counterfeiting}; on $r_{m^{-1}(i)}$ protects the carried payload too; and on $K_s$ means an adversary who edits content cannot forge a consistent authentication layer. An unkeyed, content-only hash is exactly what a published attack \cite{selfembed_attack_arxiv1812} breaks in a similar scheme; its recommended countermeasures -- keyed hashing and block interdependence -- are exactly what \eqref{eq:hmac} and the mapping below build in.

\textbf{Found-and-fixed: the recovery descriptor was unauthenticated.} An earlier version of \eqref{eq:hmac} bound the tag only to $\mathrm{ID}$, $i$, and $\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}_i)$, omitting $r_{m^{-1}(i)}$ -- leaving 96 of every block's 128 stored bits with no integrity protection. Verified exploit: flipping the two LSBs of pixels 16--63 in every block destroys every recovery descriptor at 40.29~dB PSNR, yet the old detector flagged 0 of 4096 blocks, certifying a gutted image as authentic. The fix costs zero payload bits -- the tag now also covers the carried descriptor -- and the on-disk magic was bumped (\texttt{WGT1}$\to$\texttt{WGT2}) so old watermarks fail loudly against the fixed verifier. Post-fix, the identical attack flags 4096 of 4096 blocks; imperceptibility is unchanged, since the fix changes only what is hashed, not what is embedded.

\subsection{Key-Seeded Block Mapping}

The mapping $m:\{1,\dots,K\}\to\{1,\dots,K\}$ determines which block stores which recovery descriptor: $r_i$ is embedded in block $m(i)$. It must satisfy \textbf{(R1) Bijectivity} -- $m$ is a permutation, so every 96-bit slot is exactly filled; \textbf{(R2) Spatial separation} -- $\lVert c(i)-c(m(i))\rVert_2 \ge d_{\min}$ for all $i$, so a localized attack of diameter smaller than $d_{\min}$ cannot cover both a block and the block holding its descriptor (we set $d_{\min} = \tfrac{1}{4}\min(M,N)$); and \textbf{(R3) Absence of short cycles} -- $m$ must be a \emph{single cycle} of length $K$, forbidding fixed points and mutual pairs $\{i, m(i)\}$ with $m(m(i))=i$, eliminating the two-block instance of the \emph{tamper coincidence problem} \cite{ausr3}.

The classical construction $m(i) = ((k\cdot i + t)\bmod K) + 1$, $\gcd(k,K)=1$, satisfies (R1) cheaply but has weak security and does not guarantee (R2). Our construction: (1) seed a CSPRNG with $K_s \Vert \mathrm{ID}$; (2) draw a uniformly random cyclic permutation via Fisher--Yates, defining $m$ directly as a single $K$-cycle, satisfying (R1) and (R3) exactly; (3) apply a bounded, monotone separation-repair pass that transposes cycle successors until (R2) holds everywhere, capped and deterministically retried on failure. Both sender and verifier regenerate $m$ from $(K_s,\mathrm{ID},K)$; it is never transmitted.

\textbf{Found-and-fixed: the seed order gave away more than $d_{\min}$.} An earlier version seeded step (2) with a fixed quadrant interleave, adopted only to make step (3)'s repair converge faster. Measured, this leaked structure beyond the stated $d_{\min}$ constraint: a block's partner fell diagonally opposite 47.75\% of the time against a 29.32\% separation-only baseline -- never threatening authentication, but letting an attacker with no key bias a coverage-denial attack toward a predictable diagonal partner. The optimisation proved unnecessary: a flat keyed shuffle violates (R2) on only 15\% of cycle edges, and the same repair pass clears every violation in $\approx0.1$s. It was removed; the map now measures 30.08\% diagonal-opposite, indistinguishable from the 29.32\% baseline. Not free: Section~\ref{sec:results} quantifies the cost in $\rho$, roughly 1.5 points at every ratio.

\subsection{Embedding Procedure}

Given $I$, $K_s$, $\mathrm{ID}$, the watermarked image $I_w$ is produced in one pass: (1) partition $I$ and compute $\mathbf{b}_i^{\mathrm{msb}}=\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}_i)$; (2) compute $r_i$ and $a_i$ from $\mathbf{b}_i^{\mathrm{msb}}$; (3) generate $m$ and $m^{-1}$; (4) form payload $p_i = a_i \,\Vert\, r_{m^{-1}(i)}$; (5) write $p_i$ into the two LSBs of $\mathbf{b}_i^{\mathrm{msb}}$.

\textbf{Distortion at a full-entropy payload.} Treating the two embedded LSBs as independent and uniform on $\{0,1,2,3\}$ -- a full-entropy payload -- the per-pixel error satisfies $\mathbb{E}[e^2] = 2\,\mathrm{Var}(U\{0,3\}) = 2.5$, giving
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PSNR}_{U} = 10\log_{10}\frac{255^2}{2.5} \approx 44.15\ \text{dB}.
\label{eq:psnrmax}
\end{equation}
This is a reference point, not an upper bound: real descriptors are not exactly uniform (Section~\ref{sec:results} measures each variant's deviation), but a measured PSNR far from $\mathrm{PSNR}_U$ on a synthetic full-entropy payload is a useful correctness check for a projection or serialization defect.

\subsection{Tamper Detection and Mask Generation}

Given a received image $I'$, the same key and $\mathrm{ID}$: (1) extract stored tag $\hat{a}_i$ and stored descriptor $\hat{r}_{m^{-1}(i)}$ from each block's 128 LSBs; (2) recompute $a'_i$ from $\mathrm{MSB}(\mathbf{b}'_i)$ via \eqref{eq:hmac}; (3) form the raw indicator
\begin{equation}
d_i = \begin{cases} 1, & \hat{a}_i \ne a'_i \\ 0, & \hat{a}_i = a'_i \end{cases}
\label{eq:rawindicator}
\end{equation}
(4) apply a neighbourhood refinement pass, correcting isolated positives (likely bit-error false alarms) and isolated negatives (a block whose $\mathrm{MSB}$ content happened to survive despite all its neighbours failing):
\begin{equation}
d^{\ast}_i = \begin{cases} 0, & d_i=1 \text{ and } S_i=0 \\ 1, & d_i=0 \text{ and } S_i \ge \theta_i \\ d_i, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\label{eq:refined}
\end{equation}
where $S_i = \sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}_8(i)} d_k$, $|\mathcal{N}_8(i)|$ is the count of blocks adjacent to $i$ (8 interior, 5 edge, 3 corner), and the fill threshold scales with it, $\theta_i = \left\lceil \tau\cdot|\mathcal{N}_8(i)|/8 \right\rceil$, $\tau=7$ (a fixed 7 would make corner blocks impossible to fill, silently exempting the border).

\textbf{The isolated-positive branch of \eqref{eq:refined} is disabled by default, on measured grounds.} It exists to suppress bit-error false alarms, but the null condition ($1{,}802{,}240$ block verifications) produced identically zero false positives, so the branch can only remove a \emph{true} positive; it also erases single-block tampers ($S_i=0$) entirely -- 64 pixels at $B=8$, ample to alter a digit on a financial instrument. We retain the isolated-\emph{negative} fill (closes holes inside a tampered region) and disable the isolated-positive clear by default (\texttt{clear\_isolated}).

The isolated-negative fill is not free: unlike the positive clear, it can \emph{manufacture} a detection, flipping a block positive because its neighbours are, regardless of its own tag comparison. Across the full grid this happened to five blocks out of 7{,}534{,}263 checks (\texttt{n\_refinement\_flagged}~=~5): each was authentic on its own terms, yet recovery irreversibly overwrote it. Neighbourhood smoothing inherited from soft-decision detectors is not automatically appropriate to a hard-decision cryptographic one, where the false-alarm rate it exists to suppress is provably zero.

Then (5) expand to the pixel-level mask
\begin{equation}
T(x,y) = d^{\ast}_{\beta(x,y)}, \quad \beta(x,y) = \left\lfloor\frac{y}{B}\right\rfloor\cdot\frac{N}{B} + \left\lfloor\frac{x}{B}\right\rfloor + 1
\label{eq:maskexpand}
\end{equation}
Localization resolution is intrinsically $B\times B$.

\subsection{Content Recovery}

Recovery proceeds over the flagged set $\mathcal{T}=\{i : d^{\ast}_i=1\}$. Block $i$'s descriptor resides in block $m(i)$, so recovering $i$ requires $m(i)$ to be authentic:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{b}}_i = \begin{cases}
\tilde{\mathbf{b}}_i(\hat{r}_i \text{ from } \mathbf{b}'_{m(i)}), & i\in\mathcal{T},\ d^{\ast}_{m(i)}=0 \\
\text{unrecoverable}, & i\in\mathcal{T},\ d^{\ast}_{m(i)}=1 \\
\mathbf{b}'_i, & i \notin \mathcal{T}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
Recovered blocks have their two LSBs zeroed, marking them as reconstructed rather than authentic -- a restored region must never re-authenticate as original. Let $\mathcal{U}=\{i\in\mathcal{T} : d^{\ast}_{m(i)}=1\}$ denote blocks lost to the \emph{tamper coincidence problem} \cite{ausr3} -- a flagged block whose designated partner is itself flagged; the \textbf{recoverability rate} is
\begin{equation}
\rho = 1 - \frac{|\mathcal{U}|}{|\mathcal{T}|}
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Aminuddin \emph{et al.} \cite{tcbr_tcbd} independently name and formalize this phenomenon as the Tamper Coincidence Block Rate (TCBR) and Detection (TCBD): $\rho \equiv \mathrm{TCBR}$ under Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho} and $|\mathcal{U}|/|\mathcal{T}| = 1-\mathrm{TCBR}$ is their TCBD; we keep our own notation for continuity with $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{T}$ above. For a uniformly random mapping and tampered fraction $\alpha$, $\rho \approx 1-\alpha$; (R2) forces $\rho=1$ exactly for attacks more compact than $d_{\min}$, and (R3) removes the short-cycle instance for dispersed attacks. The mapping design, not the descriptor design, sets the recovery-coverage ceiling.

\textbf{Computational complexity.} Embedding and verification are each $O(MN)$: one HMAC evaluation and one $8\times8$ DCT (or sixteen $2\times2$ means) per block, plus $O(K)$ for mapping generation, no iteration, optimization, or training. The reference implementation (NumPy + OpenCV) processes a $512\times512$ grayscale image end-to-end in tens of milliseconds on a commodity CPU, no accelerator.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

The system comprises four decoupled stages so the tamper simulator and the detector share no state. \textbf{Stage 1 (embedding).} Block Partitioner $\rightarrow$ MSB Projector \eqref{eq:msb} $\rightarrow$ \{Descriptor Encoder, Authentication Tag Generator\} $\rightarrow$ Mapping Generator (CSPRNG, single-cycle permutation, separation repair) $\rightarrow$ Payload Assembler $\rightarrow$ Dual-LSB Embedder $\rightarrow$ watermarked image $I_w$ (lossless PNG/TIFF) plus side information $\mathrm{ID}$; the key never leaves the signer. \textbf{Stage 2 (tamper simulation, evaluation only).} Applies a parameterized attack at target area ratio $\alpha$, emitting the tampered image $I'$ and the exact ground-truth mask $G$, with no access to $K_s$, $m$, or watermark internals.

\textbf{Stage 3 (detection and recovery).} Block Partitioner $\rightarrow$ Payload Extractor $\rightarrow$ Mapping Regeneration $\rightarrow$ Tag Comparison \eqref{eq:rawindicator} $\rightarrow$ Mask Refinement \eqref{eq:refined} $\rightarrow$ Pixel Mask Expansion \eqref{eq:maskexpand} $\rightarrow$ Recovery Engine \eqref{eq:recovery}. Outputs: predicted mask $T$, recovered image $I_r$, unrecoverable set $\mathcal{U}$, $\rho$, and a per-block audit log -- stored tag, recomputed tag, partner index, and a refusal reason per unrecoverable block.

\textbf{Stage 4 -- Evaluation module.} Computes imperceptibility on $(I,I_w)$, confusion-matrix metrics on $(T,G)$ at pixel and block granularity, and recovery metrics on $(I,I_r)$ restricted to the support of $G$, all seeded and bit-reproducible.

Stage 1 runs at or near acquisition (camera firmware, scanner driver, DICOM ingest gateway); Stage 3 runs at any later verification point, needing only the image, the key, and $\mathrm{ID}$ -- no original, no reference database, no network call, no model weights.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Image Corpus}

Because ground truth is generated by our own tamper harness, no annotated forgery dataset is required. The corpus is 32 colour images, 8~bpp per channel: 8 USC-SIPI Miscellaneous-set images \cite{uscsipi} at $512\times512$, and 24 Kodak PCD0992 images (18 at $768\times512$ landscape, 6 at $512\times768$ portrait). All images are stored losslessly as PNG and SHA-256 pinned in a committed manifest, so any silent change to the corpus between runs is a hard error; any lossy stage would destroy the watermark by design.

Provenance: the USC-SIPI and Kodak-PCD0992 sets as redistributed by the AuSR reference authors (github.com/girfa/ColorImageDatasets), so our images are the same bytes the AuSR series \cite{ausr1,ausr2,ausr3} keys its published per-image tables to. USC-SIPI is licensed for research/education use with attribution; Kodak PCD0992 was released by Eastman Kodak for unrestricted use.

\subsection{Tamper Simulation Protocol}

Four attack classes, each at a nominal target tamper-area ratio $\alpha\in\{10\%,20\%,\dots,70\%\}$ -- seven points, not the three coarser buckets an earlier grid used, so a real trend in $\rho(\alpha)$ cannot hide between sparse samples -- with randomized shapes and positions; achieved area is reported alongside each row rather than assumed equal to $\alpha$. Classes: (i)~\textbf{copy-paste splicing} from a different watermarked image, where index/ID-binding of \eqref{eq:hmac} is load-bearing against \cite{holliman2000counterfeiting}; (ii)~\textbf{object removal by inpainting} (OpenCV Telea/Navier--Stokes), historically the hardest case for recall since a smooth fill can leave MSB planes numerically unchanged (Section~\ref{sec:results}); (iii)~\textbf{crop-and-refill} via mirrored/tiled neighbouring texture; (iv)~\textbf{random block corruption}, a sanity control expected near-perfect. A \textbf{null condition} ($\alpha=0$) over all 32 images measures the false-positive rate, which must be identically zero given the deterministic construction.

\subsection{Metrics}

Following the transparency / tamper-detection / content-recovery axes of the survey literature \cite{eurasip2019survey}, we report all three. Imperceptibility: PSNR and SSIM \cite{wang2004image} between $I$ and $I_w$. Localization: precision, recall, F1, IoU between predicted mask $T$ and ground truth $G$, at pixel and block granularity. Recovery: PSNR/SSIM inside the tampered support $\Omega=\{(x,y):G(x,y)=1\}$, alongside $\rho$ from \eqref{eq:rho}, since excluding unrecoverable blocks inflates in-region PSNR if $\rho$ is not disclosed jointly.

\subsection{Implementation and Reproducibility}

Implemented in Python 3 (NumPy, OpenCV for block DCT and inpainting, \texttt{hashlib}/\texttt{hmac} for \eqref{eq:hmac}) -- no ML framework, no trained parameters. Free parameters $B$, descriptor variant, $d_{\min}$, $\tau$ are fixed a priori; all stochastic elements are seeded, single-threaded, no GPU. Colour images share one block mapping $m$ across channels, with independent per-channel tags/descriptors and a channel-OR detection mask -- a tamper touching one channel flags all three, at the cost of recovery overwriting untouched channels too. Block-level ground truth is an OR-reduction, matching the detector's own quantization. SSIM uses \texttt{data\_range=255}, scikit-image's default $7\times7$ uniform window; colour SSIM is the mean of per-channel SSIM.

\textbf{LSB shifting closes the gap to AuSR1.} Equation~\eqref{eq:psnrmax}'s 44.15~dB is the reference PSNR for a full-entropy payload under \emph{plain replacement}. AuSR1-style \emph{LSB shifting} moves a pixel to the nearest value whose low two bits already equal the desired payload, cutting expected squared error from 2.5 to 1.5. An earlier version could not use shifting: a shift can cross into an adjacent quantization window and change bit~2, inside the six MSB planes \eqref{eq:hmac} authenticates, so a shifted \emph{tag} pixel would make the verifier hash a different value than the embedder signed. The fix shifts only the 48 \emph{descriptor}-carrying pixels per block, never the 16 \emph{tag} pixels, since descriptor bits are already fixed and can be folded into the MSB content \emph{before} hashing, while tag bits are computed \emph{from} that folded content. The mixed-mode full-entropy reference point is $\mathrm{E}[e^2] = (16\cdot2.5 + 48\cdot1.5)/64 = 1.75$, giving 45.70~dB -- within 0.2~dB of the measured 45.479~dB (A) / 45.492~dB (C), SSIM minimum 0.98031 (Table~\ref{tab:imperceptibility}), against AuSR1's published 45.57~dB / SSIM 0.9972 / precision 0.9943. The null-condition zero-false-positive result confirms the fix did not reopen the failure mode it avoids.

\textbf{SSIM remains genuinely content-dependent.} SSIM's structure and contrast terms are normalized by local variance, so a fixed-variance embedding perturbation is comparatively larger on smooth, low-variance content than on strongly textured content. Checked against a windowing artefact: scikit-image's default $7\times7$ uniform window, the Wang \emph{et al.} \cite{wang2004image} $11\times11$ Gaussian window, and a Gaussian window on luma agree to within $0.001$ on this corpus. Variants A and C remain close to each other, since both are DCT descriptors differing mainly in per-block quantization-mode selection (Section~\ref{sec:method}).

\subsubsection{Localization Behaviour}

\textbf{Block-level precision, footnote.}\footnote{Ground truth at block granularity is the OR-reduction of the pixel-level tamper region -- a block is tampered if it covers \emph{any} tampered pixel -- so a block-level false positive is structurally impossible, and a detector flagging the entire image regardless of content would also score 1.000. Measured block-level precision is 1.000000 with zero variance; we do not headline it for exactly this reason.} The headline figure is instead \textbf{pixel-level precision}, which genuinely varies: mean \textbf{0.9614} across the full seven-ratio main grid. The shortfall from 1.0 is $8\times8$ block quantization: a flagged block is reported whole even when only part of it was altered, contributing clean pixels that score as false positives at pixel granularity. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} quantifies the resolution/payload tension: reducing $B$ from 8 to 4 raises pixel-level precision and IoU from 0.9527 to 0.9794 (+2.7 points) on the USC-SIPI subset, at the authentication cost in Table~\ref{tab:falseaccept}; prior work addresses this by verifying at multiple granularities simultaneously \cite{lin2005hierarchical}.

\textbf{Recall, and the one class that misses.} For a block whose content changed in any of bit-planes 2--7, the tag-mismatch probability is $1-2^{-32}$, so any recall deficit isolates to a block the attack wrote to without altering its six MSB planes -- characteristically a smooth inpainting fill over already-flat content. Binding $r_{m^{-1}(i)}$ into the authenticated message (Eq.~\eqref{eq:hmac}) closes most of this surface, since such a tamper still perturbs the block's LSBs. Measured block recall on the seven-ratio main grid (1,792 tamper rows): splice, crop\_refill and noise are each exactly \textbf{1.000000}; \textbf{object removal by inpainting is the sole exception}, at \textbf{0.999981} (minimum 0.998498), with \textbf{22 MSB-preserved-miss blocks} against zero on an earlier, smaller grid. This is not a regression -- it is the fix's own predicted residual, appearing in measurement for the first time on this denser 896-trial inpainting grid: at the boundary of a removal region, a smooth fill can, rarely, leave a block's full byte values -- MSB content \emph{and} its two stored LSB payload bits -- completely unchanged even though ground truth marks it tampered; with nothing written to that block, its tag cannot fail. An intrinsic, bounded residual, not a reopened security gap.

Table~\ref{tab:null} reports the executed check: zero false-positive blocks across all $1{,}802{,}240$ independent block verifications (32 images $\times$ 2 variants $\times$ 5 keys), and a rule-of-three 95\% upper bound of $1.66\times10^{-6}$ on the per-block \emph{false-positive} (Type~I) rate. This says nothing about the per-block \emph{false-accept} (Type~II) rate: the null condition contains zero tampered blocks by construction, so the $2^{-32}$ false-accept probability (Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget}) remains a property of \eqref{eq:hmac}'s output width, not something this experiment measures. Nor are these $1.8\times10^{6}$ evaluations independent Bernoulli trials: the received image is the identical in-memory array the embedder returned, so a false positive would require \eqref{eq:hmac} to disagree with itself on identical input. The honest description is a \textbf{determinism and implementation-consistency check}: a content-dependent-but-key-independent bug would reproduce identically across all five keys, whereas a genuine cryptographic property would not correlate with key choice. No such bug was observed; disk round-tripping is not exercised here.

\textbf{The recoverability curve does not collapse.} $\rho$ is governed by the mapping: $\rho\approx1$ below $d_{\min}$, $\rho\approx1-\alpha$ for dispersed attacks (Eq.~\eqref{eq:rho}). An earlier three-point grid ($\alpha\in\{10\%,25\%,50\%\}$) was too coarse to distinguish a smooth decline from a cliff. The seven-point grid (Table~\ref{tab:rhoalpha}) resolves this: $\rho$ is monotone and near-linear across 10--70\%, no knee, sitting \textbf{3--6 points above} its own bound $1-\alpha$ at every ratio -- not, and we do not claim, parity with reference-sharing schemes at high $\alpha$ (below). The two highest nominal ratios are nominal only: the region generator cannot reliably place one compact region over 60--70\% of a frame, so 0.60 achieves $0.573\pm0.046$ and 0.70 achieves $0.653\pm0.064$ -- read as ``targeted,'' not ``delivered.''

\textbf{The block-mapping structural-leak fix (Section~\ref{sec:method}) has a measured recoverability cost, the price of the security property, not a regression.} Before removing the quadrant-interleave seed order, the identical measurement gave $\rho$ roughly 1.5 points higher at every ratio, because the leaky interleave biased a block's partner toward the diagonally opposite (typically farther, hence safer) quadrant -- an artefact an attacker with no key could otherwise exploit to make a coverage-denial attack more reliable.

\textbf{In-region recovery quality is flat -- confirming that coverage sets the ceiling, not descriptor fidelity.} PSNR$_\Omega$ (Table~\ref{tab:rhoalpha}, rightmost column) stays in a narrow \textbf{29.3--29.8~dB} band across $\alpha=10\%$--$70\%$ (mean \textbf{29.00~dB}), while $\rho$ falls by a factor of 2.7 over the same range. Descriptor fidelity is a property of a single reconstructed block, essentially independent of how many blocks needed reconstruction; whole-image quality depends on \emph{how much} had to be reconstructed, which is what $\rho$ and $\alpha$ jointly set. Seven flat points, not one or two, makes this a confirmed structural finding.

A one-to-one partner mapping still fails \emph{all-or-nothing} at the block level: the tamper coincidence problem removes a whole block's worth of recovery at once, whereas reference-sharing and fountain-coded allocations \cite{zhang2011reference,korus2013efficient} spread each block's recovery information across many partners and degrade gracefully instead -- why Korus and Dziech report 37~dB whole-image reconstruction at 50\% damage against our measured 17.4~dB whole-image unmarked at the same ratio (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). Closing this gap (Future Scope item~1) is the highest-value single upgrade, distinct from the $\rho$-vs-bound finding above, which concerns the mapping against \emph{its own} structural limit.

\textbf{Marking convention.} Our default output marks unrecoverable blocks explicitly rather than fabricating content, at a whole-image PSNR cost relative to leaving those pixels at their received value: at $\alpha=50\%$, marked whole-image PSNR is 12.4~dB against 17.4~dB unmarked (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). Published recovery figures are, as best we can determine, whole-image and unmarked, so we report both: \emph{marked} for actual deployed output, \emph{unmarked} for literature comparability -- neither to be confused with the stricter \emph{in-region} figure above.

\textbf{Fidelity} (PSNR$_\Omega$, in-region on recovered blocks only) is governed by the descriptor rate: 96 bits for 64 pixels (1.5~bpp), of which Variant A's twelve-coefficient DCT truncation (Section~\ref{sec:method}) discards all high-frequency content. Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative} shows a representative end-to-end example. The flat 29.3--29.8~dB band (Table~\ref{tab:rhoalpha}) is a faithful \emph{low-frequency} reconstruction, visibly softer than its surroundings, with fine texture and sharp edges not returning -- for the target applications (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}) the correct trade: a verifier needs to know what was there approximately and verifiably, not be shown a sharp fabrication.

Reporting $\rho$ and PSNR$_\Omega$ together (Table~\ref{tab:rhoalpha}) is deliberate: a reader cannot otherwise tell whether a healthy in-region figure reflects strong reconstruction or simply a small, easy-to-recover flagged set. Here it is the stronger finding: $\rho$ falls by a factor of 2.7 over the grid while PSNR$_\Omega$ moves by under 0.5~dB -- direct evidence that whole-image recovery quality is governed by coverage, not descriptor fidelity.

\subsection{Comparison, Explainability, and Limitations}

\subsubsection{Comparison to Learned Methods}

``Detects and recovers'' is no longer a distinguishing claim: EditGuard \cite{editguard} and DeepMark \cite{deepmark} both localize and recover with a trained network, and RecoverMark \cite{recovermark} targets recovery specifically. A direct numeric comparison against passive learned localizers \cite{zhou2018learning,wu2019mantranet} is not offered either, since those localize manipulation in images never seen before embedding, which this system cannot do. The axis of difference against the learned \emph{proactive} family is \textbf{auditability}: every decision here is a single recomputed HMAC comparison, reproducible bit-for-bit by any third party holding the key, with no model weights and no run-to-run variance. This scheme needs capture-time cooperation and a lossless container, but no training data, no GPU, no model artefact to version, deterministic $O(MN)$ cost, and exact block-level localization with a per-block audit trail -- decisive where the acquisition pipeline is under institutional control (medical archives, evidence management, insurance intake); elsewhere it is a deployment decision, not a strict dominance relation.

\textbf{Localization granularity: an explicit trade-off, not an oversight.} The AuSR family \cite{ausr1,ausr2,ausr3} localizes at $2\times2$ block granularity, four times finer than the $8\times8$ used here. This is a consequence of payload capacity: at block size $B$ the two-LSB capacity is $2B^2$ bits, so $B=8$ gives 128 bits (32-bit tag plus 96-bit descriptor) while $B=4$ gives only 32, which a tag alone consumes, leaving nothing for a descriptor (Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget}). Finer localization thus costs authentication strength and recovery fidelity here -- a considered choice given the joint goal, not an oversight.

\textbf{Tag width, not block size, sets the false-accept probability.} $2^{-t}$ for a $t$-bit tag depends only on $t$ (Table~\ref{tab:falseaccept}); \emph{this implementation} couples $t$ to $B$ only because its budget is a fixed $2B^2$ bits split between tag and descriptor (Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget}), forcing $t=16$ at $B=4$ and $t=32$ at $B=8$. Detection is a channel-OR over three independently-keyed channels, so a three-channel tamper is missed only if all three tags collide, $2^{-3t}$: $2^{-48}$ at $B=4$, $2^{-96}$ at $B=8$, not the naive single-channel $2^{-16}$/$2^{-32}$. Predicted false accepts across the ablation grid are $\approx1.6\times10^{-9}$, and measurement agrees: zero misses of any kind. A finer grid is thus not simply ``better localization'': it is a strictly worse authentication posture traded for sharper boundaries.

Explainability is a concrete, not rhetorical, property: the output for each block is its stored tag, its recomputed tag, and its partner block's index, compared by bitwise inequality with no threshold to tune post hoc and no saliency map requiring interpretation; for a block found unrecoverable, an explicit refusal reason is recorded rather than a silent gap. Every field in that record is reproducible identically by any third party holding the key.

\subsubsection{Threat Model}

\textbf{Adversary.} Holds the watermarked image but not $K_s$, and can apply arbitrary pixel-domain edits before verification; may know the full algorithm (Kerckhoffs's principle). \textbf{Out of scope:} an adversary possessing $K_s$; one who edits \emph{before} embedding; lossy recompression, a deployment-discipline requirement (lossless storage), not a cryptographic one. \textbf{Guarantee:} any modification to bit-planes 2--7 of any block is detected with probability $1-2^{-32}$ per channel (Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget}), regardless of algorithm knowledge, as long as $K_s$ is secret. \textbf{Key compromise voids all guarantees}: an adversary holding $K_s$ can compute valid tags and descriptors for manipulated content and re-embed it so the result verifies perfectly -- the necessary consequence of a symmetric-key, deterministic construction, not a patchable defect.

\textbf{Limitations, stated plainly.} No robustness to lossy processing; the scheme applies only to losslessly stored imagery. Two LSB planes are permanently consumed. Localization is quantized to $8\times8$ and cannot improve without a finer grid (Table~\ref{tab:falseaccept} gives its authentication cost). Recovery is low-frequency only, bounded by a 1.5~bpp budget. $\rho$ tracks $1-\alpha$ from above rather than collapsing toward it (Table~\ref{tab:rhoalpha}), but one-to-one backup allocation is still all-or-nothing relative to reference-sharing schemes at high $\alpha$; and the block-mapping seed order must not leak structure beyond the stated $d_{\min}$ guarantee -- the quadrant-interleave order originally used did exactly this (partner diagonally opposite 47.75\% of the time against a 29.32\% baseline) and was removed at a measured $\approx1.5$-point $\rho$ cost. The tag \eqref{eq:hmac} covers a block's complete 128-bit payload, so tampering with either half is detected; it does not cover whether a flagged block can be \emph{reconstructed}, which depends on its mapped partner also being intact.

\section{Conclusion and Future Scope}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented a complete, deterministic, training-free framework for watermark-guided tamper localization and recovery based on self-embedding fragile watermarking. The design partitions the image into $8\times8$ blocks and allocates the 128 bits in two LSB planes between a 32-bit keyed tag stored in-block and a 96-bit recovery descriptor stored in a distant partner block, computed from the six MSB planes so the construction is self-consistent without iteration. Keying the tag to block index and image identifier defends against block-wise collage counterfeiting \cite{holliman2000counterfeiting}; the key-seeded single-cycle permutation with enforced minimum separation avoids the tamper coincidence problem \cite{ausr3}, guaranteeing full recoverability for attacks more compact than $d_{\min}$.

The methodological contribution is the joint evaluation protocol: over four tamper classes at seven ratios (10--70\%), with exact self-generated ground truth, imperceptibility, localization, recoverability rate, and in-region recovery fidelity are reported together, a trade-off the literature has often reported one half at a time. Measured over the full grid (3{,}232 rows, 25 of 27 sanity checks passing), the headline results (Section~\ref{sec:results}) confirm coverage, not descriptor fidelity, governs whole-image recovery -- and closing the gap to reference-sharing schemes' graceful degradation (Future Scope item 1) is the highest-value next step.

Target applications include forensic evidence archives, journalism/media authentication at ingest, medical imaging integrity in PACS, legal document scans, insurance claim verification, and self-authenticating camera/device firmware.

Extensions follow directly. \textbf{(1) Reference sharing} \cite{zhang2011reference,korus2013efficient}: a shared-reference or fountain-coded allocation in place of one-to-one backup would raise the tolerable tamper ratio beyond $1-\alpha$ -- the highest-value single upgrade. \textbf{Hierarchical multi-scale verification} \cite{lin2005hierarchical} would decouple localization resolution from per-block payload. \textbf{Semi-fragile extension} \cite{linchang2000} would tolerate a bounded, declared degree of JPEG re-compression. \textbf{Reversible embedding} would allow bit-exact restoration of verified-authentic images. \textbf{Rate-adaptive descriptors} could allocate more bits to salient blocks under a global budget. \textbf{Colour and video} would widen the recovery-data space via inter-frame mapping. \textbf{Hybrid operation with passive forensics}, using the watermark's exact mask as a trusted prior, combines active assurance with blind-analysis coverage.
