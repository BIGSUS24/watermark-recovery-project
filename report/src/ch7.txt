The evaluation grid holds 3,232 rows in output/runs.csv. The main grid contributes 1,792 rows: 32 images, four tamper classes, seven nominal tamper ratios from 0.10 to 0.70, and two descriptor variants, A and C, at B = 8. @@SUB 7.1.2 Localization

Block-level recall is 1.0000 for copy-paste splicing, crop-and-refill and noise corruption, and 0.99998 for object removal by inpainting. Across the 1,792 main-grid trials, 17 blocks were missed because the inpainting preserved their most-significant bit-planes, the predicted residual of the descriptor-binding design and not an unexplained failure. Pixel-level precision is 0.9599 as a main-grid mean and rises with tamper ratio: 0.9248, 0.9481, 0.9581, 0.9643, 0.9701, 0.9740 and 0.9797 at ratios 0.10 through 0.70. The shortfall from 1.0 is the 8x8 block-quantization penalty, which shrinks as regions grow. Neighborhood refinement flagged only 2 blocks out of 10,092,544 verified.

Block-level precision is deliberately not headlined: a block counts as tampered if the region covers any of its pixels, so a block-level false positive is structurally impossible and the metric reads 1.000 regardless of detector quality. The pixel-level figure is the honest measure of how tightly the flagged area follows the true region.

The null condition tests unmodified watermarked images. Across 320 rows, 1,802,240 independent block verifications produced 0 false-positive blocks, a false-positive rate of 0.000000 with a rule-of-three 95% upper bound of 1.66e-6. This is a determinism and implementation-consistency check across images, variants and keys, not a measurement of the 2^-32 cryptographic false-accept probability, which no experiment of this size could resolve. @@SEC 7.2 Conclusions

This project built a deterministic, training-free self-embedding fragile watermarking scheme. Each 8x8 block carries, in its two least-significant bit-planes, a 32-bit keyed HMAC-SHA256 tag and a 96-bit descriptor of a spatially distant partner block chosen by a key-seeded single-cycle permutation. The implementation is 9,929 lines of Python across 24 modules, plus a local web application and demonstration interface, with no machine-learning framework or trained parameter.

Over 3,232 rows and 32 images, imperceptibility is 45.479 dB for Variant A, block-level recall is 1.0000 for three of four tamper classes, the null condition shows no false-positive block in 1,802,240 verifications, and recoverability is 3 to 6 percentage points above its structural bound. In-region recovery quality, at 29.81 dB, is modest, and whole-image figures are well below fountain-coded and reference-sharing schemes.

The claim is narrower than accuracy: the design offers accountability, since every verification decision is one recomputed keyed hash comparison that any third party holding the key can reproduce bit for bit. The distant-mapping idea is not claimed as new, nor is superiority over learned proactive watermarking. The scheme is fully fragile, protecting images only inside a lossless custody chain; its one-to-one backup allocation fails all-or-nothing per block, capping whole-image reconstruction at high tamper ratios; and security rests on key secrecy. Section 7.3 addresses these limits.

@@SEC 7.3 Future Work

Five directions follow from the measurements and Section 6.2.

* Reference sharing or fountain-coded descriptor allocation is the highest-value upgrade: mapping coverage, not descriptor fidelity, limits recovery quality (Section 7.1.3), so spreading each descriptor across several blocks addresses it directly.
* A semi-fragile mode tolerant of benign JPEG re-encoding would extend the scheme beyond a strictly lossless custody chain, its largest deployment restriction.
* Finer or hierarchical localization grids that do not pay the full tag cost would raise pixel-level precision beyond 0.9599 without the halved tag and false-accept penalty of B = 4.
* Integration into capture firmware and PACS or DICOM ingest would test the scheme where the applications of Section 6.3 apply and measure embedding cost on constrained hardware.
* A formal security proof of the keyed construction would replace the present argument from the standard properties of HMAC-SHA256 [20] with a stated reduction covering every bound field.
